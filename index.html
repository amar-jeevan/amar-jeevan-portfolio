About Me

My background is in electrical engineering, with graduate specialisation in numerical methods and system-level simulation. The connecting thread across my work is translating physical systems into computational models that can support engineering decisions under realistic constraints.

At Forschungszentrum Jülich, I built a Python simulation framework for cryogenic cable systems where material properties vary strongly with temperature and the available cooling power is limited. The core challenge was constructing a nonlinear steady-state solver that converged reliably across wide temperature ranges while remaining flexible enough for parameter studies and future validation. The work was conducted as part of my master's thesis under Prof. Dr.-Ing. Philipp Keil at OTH Regensburg.

Beyond cryogenics, I have hands-on experience in embedded systems (STM32, FPGA/RFSoC), power electronics, and signal processing — which gives me a practical hardware reference point alongside the simulation work.

Core Strengths

Thermal & Physics-Based Modeling

Fourier heat conduction, temperature-dependent material properties, lumped parameter modeling, and steady-state system analysis under boundary constraints — applied in cryogenic and power system contexts.
